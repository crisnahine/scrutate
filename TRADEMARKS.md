# Scrutate trademark policy

Version 1.0, 2026-11-13.

"Scrutate"™, the Scrutate logo (the lens crossed by a beam, `docs/assets/logo-mark.svg`) and
the wordmark are trademarks of Cris Nahine, the maintainer of Scrutate. They are not registered
marks today. The name has not been cleared by a lawyer (docs/NAMING.md says what was and was not
checked).

The code is under an open source licence, MIT or Apache-2.0 at your choice (LICENSE-MIT,
LICENSE-APACHE). That licence covers the code. It does not give anyone a right to the name or
the logo; this page says what you may do with them. The aim is simple: when people see
"Scrutate", they should get the software this project released, or be told plainly that they
are not.

## You may, without asking

- Use the name to say what something is, is for, or works with: "a plugin for Scrutate",
  "compatible with Scrutate", "benchmarked against Scrutate", in articles, talks, documentation
  and reviews.
- Distribute the unmodified releases of this project under the name, and package them for an
  operating system or a package manager under the name, with only the changes that packaging
  needs (paths, build flags, a version suffix of the distribution).
- Use the logo unchanged to link to the project or to show that your tool works with it, as long
  as it does not look like the project made or endorses your tool.

## Ask first

Write to the maintainer through the project's repository before you:

- Name a product, a company, a hosted service, a domain or a package "Scrutate" or anything
  close enough to be mistaken for it.
- Distribute a modified version under the name or the logo. A fork with changes beyond packaging
  needs its own name. It may say it is "based on Scrutate" or "a fork of Scrutate".
- Use the name or the logo on merchandise, or in a way that suggests sponsorship, partnership or
  endorsement.
- Change the logo, or combine it with another mark.

## Always

- Do not use the marks in a way that misleads about who made something, that disparages, or that
  breaks the law.
- When a page uses the marks, a line such as "Scrutate is a trademark of Cris Nahine" is
  appreciated.

Nothing here takes away a right the law gives you, such as naming a product to describe or
compare it. If you are unsure whether a use is fine, ask.
